Data Use

iHRIS Administrator - Level I, © IntraHealth International, CC BY 4.0. Originally offered at hrhresourcecenter.org/elearning (CapacityPlus). Licence stated by its author, the folio owner, 2026-10-09.

1. Data Use

This module outlines:

  • the purpose of a routine HRIS
  • aspects of data quality
  • the Administrator’s role in appropriate system and data use

2. Purpose of a Routine HRIS

A routine HRIS provides essential information for health workforce decision-making.

If the data is not available when needed, not of good quality, or not being utilized for practical policy, management, and staffing projection decisions—then the HRIS is not meeting its purpose for the organization.

The role as Administrator is to ensure all of these criteria are addressed.

3. Data Quality

There are several factors that together comprise data quality:

  • consistency: standards should be used to ensure all like information is captured and categorized together
  • completeness: system-wide reports are only possible if all the information is in the database
  • correctness: accurate reports are not possible if the data is captured incorrectly or not at all
  • timeliness: current decisions cannot be made with out-of-date information
  • When it is not possible to provide reports with real-time data, each report should reflect a date stamp to avoid any potential confusion about the timeliness of data.
  • accessibility: if the right people cannot access the information when needed, the system is not worthwhile
  • Administrators ensure this by granting decision-makers direct access to the system or establishing a formal process outlining how to request it and expectations about response times.

4. Data Quality

  • relevance: if the data is not pertinent to the organization then it serves no purpose
  • an Administrator helps users understand there should be a purpose for data that is collected and assist in outlining what information to include:
    • Why do you want this information?
    • How often do you anticipate needing this information?
    • What decision will this information help you to make?
    • What is the impact of NOT having this information available?
    • Who should provide this information?
    • How often should it be collected?
    • How often should it be reported and to whom?
    • Does the purpose and usefulness of this information justify the time and expense involved in collecting, reporting, and reviewing it?

5. Access Issues - Training

Administrators must ensure the right people have access to the data they require.

One way to do this is training HR data clerks, HR managers, and other data consumers to use the system and produce the reports, including developing appropriate training curriculum, materials and a retraining schedule for new employees.

Recommended training materials:

  • data dictionary to define HRIS fields: misinterpretations of fields are common and can lead to confusion on the meaning of reports (see the CapacityPlus data dictionary for the core iHRIS Manage software http://open.intrahealth.org/mediawiki/iHRIS:Data_Dictionary)
  • user manual: screen shots and instructions for every task a user must perform in the system (see the CapacityPlus user manual for iHRIS Manage software http://www.capacityproject.org/hris/hris-toolkit/tools/pdf/iHRISManageUserManual.pdf)

6. Access Issues—Single Interaction Point

If an organization prefers the Administrator to be the primary point of interaction for HRIS reporting, then you must establish procedures for access and requesting information:

  • how to request a report (form, email, etc )
  • how much time is required to meet a request
  • restrictions on report requests if there is sensitive information available only to managers

Administrators must also help users to specify what data and reports they need:

  • needed reports—when, whom, and how often
  • format requirements
  • purpose of the information
  • information a field references—for example, should "Date of Service" refer to the date a person joined the organization or the date a person started in a particular position
  • time parameters for each report (start date and end dates)
  • summary needs—summary for the entire date range, only the most current data, or a listing of data available at various points within this date range

7. Guiding Users

Administrators must also guide users unfamiliar with HRIS to understand which reports would be most useful for their purposes.

This requires learning how to take user policy and management questions and map them to data elements in the HRIS to meet the user’s needs.

8. Guiding Users Example

The Director of Personnel asks for assistance in identifying staff who have held the same position for more than 12 months and completed in-service training and are thus qualified for a promotion.

  • pull a report on persons who have been in the same post for 12 months or longer
  • pull a report on those who have completed in-service training in the last 12 months
  • pull a position vacancy report to inform on the possible positions available for staff advancement
